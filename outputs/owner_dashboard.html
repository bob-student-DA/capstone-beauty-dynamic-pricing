Your busiest hours could earn you so much more

Looking back at almost a year of appointments for a salon owner, some hours fill up again and again while others sit half-empty. Charging a little more when you are in demand can raise your revenue without losing many clients.

What to do next

  1. Start small. Add about 10% to color services at 9 am, 10 am, 12 pm for 6 to 8 weeks. Keep a few busy slots at your usual price so regulars still have a choice.
  2. Fill quiet times with short offers, not permanent discounts. A limited-time deal for 1 pm, 3 pm, 5 pm (for example, "book by Friday") moves clients without lowering what everyone else pays.
  3. Cut no-shows on haircuts. About 1 in 4 haircut and blonding bookings cancel or no-show. A small deposit or a reminder text will protect more income than a all over price change.

By service

Color services

All over color, full balayage, root touch-up with cut

Six in ten of these bookings land in your busiest hours, and clients rarely cancel.

Try: a 10% busy-hour increase. This is where it makes the biggest difference.

Planned-ahead services

Partial highlights, gloss with haircut, partial balayage, and other services

Clients book about 4 weeks ahead, so most can pick another time if busy hours cost more.

Try: a modest busy-hour increase. Expect clients to change times rather than leave.

Highlights

Full and mini custom highlights

Most of these are already booked at quieter times.

Try: featuring these in quiet-time offers to fill empty afternoon slots.

Haircuts and blonding

How these estimates work
  • Based on 186 appointments from one stylist's booking log over about 44 weeks (White, 2025).
  • How clients react to price changes comes from a published study of off-peak pricing (Thommen & Hintermann, 2023), not from this salon. The range shows less and more flexible clients.
  • When busy-hour prices go up, a few clients book elsewhere and about half of them move to a quieter time instead.
  • Tested on the last 13 weeks of the log: a 20% busy-hour increase raised income by about 3% to 5%, and every version beat flat pricing.
  • Monthly numbers are weekly averages times 4.3. Small salons vary a lot week to week, so treat these as a guide and confirm with a short trial.
